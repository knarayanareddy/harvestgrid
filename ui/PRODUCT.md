# HarvestGrid — Product Truth

## Audience

Tree owners, gleaning coordinators, trained volunteers, and recipient coordinators. The primary design user is the coordinator balancing forecast uncertainty, safety, labor, and recipient capacity across many trees.

## Core job

**Move from dated Ring evidence to a reviewed, safe, well-coordinated glean and a weighed outcome—without exposing residential information.**

## Operating context

- Data-rich responsive web for coordinators, simpler owner views, and mobile day-of volunteer screens.
- Seasonal work, outdoor readability, imperfect images, uncertain forecasts, and role-specific location privacy.
- Existing organizations with real training, waivers, insurance, and operational checklists.

## Product personality

Grounded, practical, transparent, and field-ready. A field operations ledger with modern evidence tools—not a farm-game map, smart-home security console, or generic BI dashboard.

## Voice

- `Experimental forecast · coordinator review required`
- `Visible fruit: 42 estimated · 30–55 interval`
- `Ready window is not a food-safety assessment.`
- `Address hidden until assignment is confirmed.`

Avoid: `AI says ripe`, `Guaranteed yield`, `Auto-dispatch`, or unqualified food-rescue impact claims.

## Durable constraints

- Forecast uncertainty and data quality appear beside the forecast, not in a separate technical page.
- Coordinator approval and hard-filter reasons are legible.
- Owner address and media are visually partitioned from general operational data.
- Actual accepted weight and forecast error are distinct from estimated recovered impact.
- Dense views retain strong reading order, filters, and table alternatives.

## Visual anti-references

- Public residential pin maps, surveillance-style video walls, and face/plate overlays.
- Glossy agricultural stock photography as a substitute for evidence.
- Oversaturated green-everything palettes and ripe/unripe color-only states.
- Tiny KPI cards, unexplained gauges, and decorative charts without operational decisions.

## Design success signals

- A coordinator can identify the next review requiring action and why within ten seconds.
- A volunteer sees only the information needed at each assignment stage.
- Forecast intervals and actual weights cannot be confused.
- Tables and outdoor mobile views remain usable with zoom, keyboard, and imperfect connectivity.
